CONFERENCE PRESENTATION · 2025

International Conference on Life, Earth and Science for Sustainable Development (ICLESSD-2025)

Venue: Jagannath University, Dhaka, Bangladesh

Date: December 18–19, 2025

Research Abstract

Urban water bodies play a crucial role in moderating local climate and maintaining urban thermal comfort. However, rapid urban expansion in Dhaka has resulted in substantial water shrinkage, influencing surface temperature dynamics and increasing heat stress. This study investigates the relationship between water loss and Land Surface Temperature (LST) from 2000 to 2025 using Artificial Intelligence (AI) models and predicts future thermal comfort for 2030, 2035, and 2040 at 5-year intervals. Historical thermal comfort maps were generated using 11 environmental criteria and classified training samples (280 thermal and 120 cool) through the XGBoost algorithm, achieving an average accuracy of 87%. Linear Regression (LR) analysis revealed a mean ΔLST increase of 5.68 °C (5.7%) between 2000 and 2025, with a strong correlation (R² = 0.96, p = 0.0006). To forecast long-term trends, an LSTM neural network model was applied, predicting LST increases of 1.36 °C by 2030, 2.58 °C by 2035, and 3.79 °C by 2040 relative to 2025. Future thermal comfort zones predicted by LSTM indicated that “Very High” heat stress areas would increase by 0.3% by 2030, 0.4% by 2035, and 0.5% by 2040, while “High” zones would decrease by 1.7% by 2030, 2.7% by 2035, and 4.1% by 2040, reflecting a shifting pattern of critical warming zones. Model validation yielded MSE = 0.85, RMSE = 0.92, MAE = 0.75, and R² = 0.87, confirming moderate-to-high predictive accuracy. These results demonstrate the utility of AI-based models for assessing urban heat vulnerability, forecasting future thermal comfort impacts due to water loss, and guiding urban planning strategies to enhance microclimatic stability in Dhaka.
