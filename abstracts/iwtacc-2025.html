CONFERENCE PRESENTATION · 2025

International Workshop on Technologies for Adaptation to Climate Change (IWTACC-2025)

Venue: Asian Institute of Technology (AIT), Bangkok, Thailand

Date: November 21–22, 2025

Research Abstract

Climate change-induced hazards, particularly cyclones, storm surges, salinity intrusion, and coastal flooding, are increasing in frequency and intensity, making adaptation essential for reducing vulnerability and strengthening community resilience in coastal Bangladesh. This study assessed the spatial distribution of climate change adaptive capacity in Kalapara Upazila using a GIS- and Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP)-based Adaptive Capacity Index (ACI). Fourteen environmental, socio-economic, infrastructural, institutional, and land-use indicators were integrated through a Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) framework to produce a spatially explicit adaptive capacity map. The results classified the study area into five adaptive capacity classes, with Moderate adaptive capacity covering the largest area (126.59 km²; 28.45%), followed by Low adaptive capacity (114.54 km²; 25.74%). Higher adaptive capacity was concentrated in Dhankhali, Dalbuganj, Tiakhali, and Lalua, whereas Baliatali, Dhulasar, Mithaganj, and parts of Nilganj exhibited comparatively lower adaptive capacity because of greater exposure to coastal hazards and limited adaptive resources. Model validation demonstrated strong predictive performance, achieving an ROC–AUC of 0.895, an Overall Accuracy of 0.871, a Kappa coefficient of 0.740, and a low AHP Consistency Ratio (CR = 0.0094), confirming the reliability of the proposed framework. The generated adaptive capacity map provides spatial evidence to support policymakers in prioritizing adaptation investments and developing location-specific climate resilience strategies for vulnerable coastal communities.
